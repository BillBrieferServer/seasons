Services

What I do.

Personal companion care and support for seniors who want to stay independent in their own homes. Every arrangement is a little different — take what is useful and leave the rest.

Companionship

  • Friendly conversation and visiting
  • Games, puzzles, reading together
  • Outings — coffee, lunch, walks, scenic drives
  • Technology help — phone, tablet, video calls with family, email

Light housekeeping

  • Dishes, laundry, tidying
  • Changing linens
  • Light cleaning and organizing
  • Refrigerator and pantry organization

Home management

  • Waiting for service providers — plumber, cable, repairs
  • Mail sorting and organization
  • Medication reminders — a visual check only, never administering
  • Pet care — feeding, walking, vet visits

Healthy eating support

  • Weekly meal planning — heart-healthy, diabetic-friendly, gluten-free, or simply what you like
  • Grocery shopping with nutrition guidance
  • Meal preparation and batch cooking
  • Help with special dietary needs

This is the part most caregivers don't offer. It comes from eight years of health and wellness coaching.

Transportation & accompaniment

  • Medical appointments — doctor, dentist, specialists, lab work, physical therapy
  • Personal appointments — hair salon, shopping
  • Social activities — church, senior center, visiting friends and family
  • Special events — grandchildren's activities, family gatherings

I go in with you if you'd like, wait through the appointment, and take notes if that's helpful.

Errands & shopping

  • Grocery shopping — with you or for you
  • Prescription pickup
  • Post office, bank, dry cleaning
  • Returns and exchanges
  • Pet supplies

Caregiver respite

  • A real break for the family caregiver
  • Scheduled regular relief or occasional coverage
  • Updates for family members who live out of town

Being clear

What I don't provide.

To keep you safe and to stay inside the right boundaries, I do not provide:

  • Medical or nursing care — administering medication, wound care, bathing, dressing or toileting assistance
  • Heavy housework — deep cleaning, moving furniture, yard work, ladders
  • Financial management — managing accounts or signing checks

If you need any of that, tell me anyway — I am glad to help you find someone qualified.

Paying for care

Who usually pays for this?

Most families pay privately, out of pocket. Two other sources are worth checking before you assume you can't afford help:

  • VA Aid & Attendance. Veterans and surviving spouses may qualify for a monthly benefit that helps cover this kind of care.
  • Long-term care insurance. Some policies cover companion care. It is worth a call to your provider to ask.

Medicare does not cover non-medical companion care. Anyone who tells you otherwise is mistaken.
