My story

I have been where you are.

I didn't start this business because I saw a market opportunity. I started it because I have lived it.

I spent five years in Alaska, calling my parents about once a week. "We're doing fine" was the usual report. But when I moved back to Pocatello, I saw a little fuller picture... the small things a phone call could never tell me. The house wasn't quite as tidy. The groceries sat out a little longer. The things that used to be effortless and routine had simply become harder for them.

I've watched my mom walk this road with her own mother, trying to honor her independence while making sure she was safe, all while keeping up with her own home, marriage, and family. I remember going to Grandma's once a week to visit, vacuum, dust, and drop off groceries, just so Mom could have a day to breathe. It wasn't much. But it mattered. And now that Grandma is gone, that time I had with her is one of my most precious gifts.

These experiences have taught me something I didn't expect: the help that matters most at this stage is rarely medical. It's a ride to an appointment. It's real food in the refrigerator. It's someone noticing the mail has piled up and quietly handling it. It's simply showing up and caring.

Having a little extra help isn't giving up your independence. It's protecting it.

When we moved back to Pocatello, I found myself at a crossroads. A season I had poured myself into for years wasn't fitting anymore, and I had to decide whether to keep pushing forward out of habit, or step back and ask what I was really meant to be doing. When I finally gave myself permission to change course, this work was waiting for me. It felt less like starting something new and more like coming home.

So this is what I do now.

I spent eight years as a certified health and wellness coach, which is why nutrition runs through everything I offer. Good food isn't a luxury when you're eighty. It's the difference between holding steady and slipping. I plan menus, shop with purpose, and cook things that actually get eaten.

I live right here in Pocatello. I shop at the same stores and drive the same streets. When I say I'm local, I mean I'm only minutes away.

And I'm not an agency. You won't get a different stranger every week. You get me; someone who learns your name, your routine, how you take your coffee, and which grandchild is which.

A note to adult children

I know you're worried. I know you're probably doing this from a distance, or squeezing it in between your own job, your own kids, and your own life.

Let me be your eyes and ears, the extra set of hands, and the phone call that tells you how the appointment actually went. You don't have to carry all of it alone.

There comes a season when a little help makes all the difference. I'm here for yours. 🩶
