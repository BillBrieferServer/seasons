My story

I have been where you are.

I didn't start this business because I saw a market opportunity. I started it because I have sat in that waiting room myself.

I have sat at the cardiologist trying to remember all the questions I meant to ask. I have stood in a grocery aisle wondering whether my dad is eating enough protein. I have watched a house that used to feel effortless start to feel like too much.

Caring for my own parents and my grandmother taught me something I didn't expect: the help that matters most is rarely medical. It's a ride. It's real food in the refrigerator. It's someone noticing that the mail has stacked up, and quietly handling it.

So that's the work I do now.

Asking for a little help isn't giving up your independence. It's protecting it.

I spent eight years as a certified health and wellness coach before this, which is why nutrition runs through everything I offer. Good food isn't a luxury add-on when you're eighty — it's the difference between holding steady and slipping. I plan menus, shop with a purpose, and cook things that actually get eaten.

I live in Pocatello. I shop at the same stores, drive the same streets, and complain about the same winters. When I say I am local, I mean I am only minutes away.

And I'm not an agency. You won't get a different stranger every week. You get me — someone who learns your name, your routine, how you take your coffee, and which grandchild is which.

A note to adult children

I know you're worried, and I know you're probably doing this from a distance while juggling your own family and work.

Let me be your eyes and ears — the extra set of hands, and the phone call that tells you how the appointment actually went. You don't have to carry all of it alone.

I'll treat your parent the way I treat my own.
