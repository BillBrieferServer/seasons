My story

I have been where you are.

I did not start this business because I saw a market opportunity. I started it because I have sat in that waiting room myself.

I have sat at the cardiologist trying to remember all the questions I meant to ask. I have stood in a grocery aisle wondering whether my dad is eating enough protein. I have watched a house that used to feel effortless start to feel like too much.

Caring for my own parents and my grandmother taught me something I did not expect: the help that matters most is rarely medical. It is a ride. It is real food in the refrigerator. It is someone noticing that the mail has stacked up, and quietly handling it.

So that is the work I do now.

Asking for a little help isn't giving up your independence. It's protecting it.

I spent eight years as a certified health and wellness coach before this, which is why nutrition runs through everything I offer. Good food is not a luxury add-on when you are eighty — it is the difference between holding steady and slipping. I plan menus, shop with a purpose, and cook things that actually get eaten.

I live in Pocatello. I shop at the same stores, drive the same streets, and complain about the same winters. When I say I am local, I mean my truck is fifteen minutes away.

And I am not an agency. You will not get a different stranger every week. You get me — someone who learns your name, your routine, how you take your coffee, and which grandchild is which.

A note to adult children

I know you are worried, and I know you are probably doing this from a distance while juggling your own family and work.

Let me be your eyes and ears — the extra set of hands, and the phone call that tells you how the appointment actually went. You do not have to carry all of it alone.

I will treat your parent the way I treat my own.
